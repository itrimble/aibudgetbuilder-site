# Methodology

How AI Budget Build decides what to publish, how it checks itself, and how it stays current. This file is instructions
for whoever adds data (a person or an assistant). The sections marked AUTO are regenerated by `npm run methodology` from the live data
and from the rule list in `src/lib/audit.ts`, so the rules in this file cannot drift from the rules that actually run.
Version 1, started 2026-11-03.

The standard we hold ourselves to: **a reader, a vendor or a competitor can pick any number on the site, follow it to a dated source,
and either confirm it or see exactly why we labelled it as an estimate.** Authority in this niche comes from being right about small things,
visibly, and from correcting mistakes in public. It does not come from claiming to be the best.

## 1. What we claim and what we do not

We claim: what a part is, what it cost to buy on a stated date at an asking price, what other people measured with a named setup, and whether
a combination of parts physically and electrically fits, according to published specs and the rules below.

We do not claim: that a price is available to you, that a speed will match what you get, that an unmeasured combination works, or that any
part is "best". Superlatives need a date and a rule ("lowest asking price per GB among the cards listed, 2026-11-03").
If we have not tested it ourselves, the page says **not verified** on the same line as the claim.

## 2. Evidence tiers

Every record has a `source_url` and `checked_on`. The tier comes from the source host (see `tier()` in `audit.ts`).

| Tier | What it is | May support |
|-|-|-|
| A | Maker documents, standards bodies, our own copy of a manual | Specs, slot maps, power connectors |
| B | Named, dated measurements and write-ups by identifiable people or reputable publications; model pages on Hugging Face | Speeds (with caveats), model facts, how-to claims |
| C | Marketplace listings and price trackers | Asking prices only, never specs |
| D | Forum threads, comments, video comments | Leads to chase. Never shown as fact. A tier D claim must be marked "reader report, not verified" |
| M | Our own measurement, with the protocol in section 7 | Anything, labelled "measured by us" with date and setup |

Rules of use: a spec cites tier A or B. A price cites tier C and is dated. A speed cites tier B or M and carries its card count, backend and caveats.
When two sources disagree, show the more conservative number and say they disagree. Never average sources that measured different things.

## 3. How each kind of data is collected

**Prices.** Asking prices from listing pages and trackers, read on the check date, entered as a low to high range with outliers excluded and the
exclusion noted. The low end is the lowest current asking price seen on the check date; a tracker's average, median or 12-month figure is not a price you can pay today and may appear only in the note. Never quotes, never sold prices unless the source says sold. A price is stale after 14 days and may not ship after 30
(exact limits in the generated table below). Refresh weekly. A combined price (for example card plus adapter) says so in the note and is our sum.

**Specs.** From the maker's datasheet or manual where one exists. Until `spec_source_url` is filled the audit shows a warning (R08), which is published.

**Boards.** Slot lane widths and generations from the manual. Bifurcation is `unknown` unless the manual or a tested BIOS says otherwise.
Anything simplified from extracted text is labelled in the record's note.

**Models.** Parameter counts, licences and file facts from the model's Hugging Face page, opened by us. The memory estimate is our arithmetic:
parameters x bits per weight / 8, plus a context allowance, with the working shown in `vram_basis`. A measured figure replaces it and says "measured".
The audit recomputes the estimate from the stated quantisation (R12) and fails on a mismatch.

**Benchmarks.** Only rows from a named person's published results, with source, date, backend and card count (or "card count unknown"). Our own
numbers go in only under section 7. Speeds are never combined across different software versions.

**Images.** Section "Images" in `DESIGN-BIBLE.md`: our own photo, then a free-licence Commons photo with credit, then an icon tile. The audit blocks any image without a free licence, an author and a source page (R18).

**Currencies.** Every stored price is a US dollar asking price. Other currencies are a display conversion at the European Central Bank's euro reference rate, kept as a dated snapshot in `src/data/rates.json` and refreshed by `node scripts/update-rates.mjs` (the browser makes no third-party requests). Rates older than 7 days warn and older than 30 may not ship (R22 to R24). Conversion goes through EUR (USD to EUR to target) and is tested for round trips and cross-rate agreement. A converted figure always sits beside the original USD figure, and the page says it is an estimate that leaves out VAT or sales tax, import duty and shipping, and that many US sellers do not ship abroad. We never present a converted number as a local listing price.

**Budgets.** A budget covers only what the site prices: cards, adapters and risers, and a PSU when the one you have cannot carry the build. It does not cover the board, CPU, RAM, storage or case, and the page says so beside every result. Options are filtered on the top of each price range, so a result stays inside the budget even at the highest asking price. Every option runs the full compatibility checks and is dropped if any error remains, so the recommender cannot suggest a build the checks would reject. What a tier buys is computed for the visitor's own setup and never typed into tier text (R25). Results that rely on placeholder adapter or PSU prices say so (R27).

**Affiliate links.** Never change ranking, wording or order. The disclosure text is generated from what is switched on (R19).

## 4. How compatibility is decided

`src/lib/compat.ts` is the only place that decides whether a build works. Its rules are written down in the checks list on the page and tested in `compat.test.ts`:
card placement (widest real slots first, then adapters, then x1 risers), lane width warnings, physical room (more than two double-slot cards needs an open frame),
M.2 slots lost to adapters, passive cards need forced airflow, PSU watts with 20% headroom on a 150 W base allowance, 8-pin connector counts,
software stack (CUDA, ROCm, Vulkan) per card, mixed vendors need Vulkan, 32 GB cards need a large BIOS memory window (reported, not verified on our board).
These are conservative on purpose. A build the checker rejects may still work; a build it passes must not be one we know to fail.

When a check is wrong, fix the check, add a test that reproduces the wrong answer first, and log it in section 9. Example already in the log: the goal picker once said a
two-card suggestion fit while the build had three errors; suggestions now run the full checks and list what else is needed.

## 5. Adding or changing data: the steps

1. Open the source. Do not cite a page you did not open. If you only saw a search excerpt, say so in the note.
2. Add or edit the record in `src/data/...` with `source_url` and today's `checked_on`. Use the tier rules above.
3. For a **card**: set vram, tdp, slot width, connectors, cooling, stacks, price range, a note saying what you give up, and `spec_source_url` if you have one.
4. For a **board**: slots with lanes and generation from the manual, M.2 slots, RAM type and slots, bifurcation `unknown` unless shown.
5. For a **model**: parameters, quant, estimate with `vram_basis` starting "My arithmetic" or "Measured", licence, source.
6. For a **benchmark**: row plus source, backend, card count, caveats. Do not add a row you cannot trace.
7. For a **preset**: ids must exist, and the number in its name must equal the cards' total (R11).
8. Run `npm test`. Fix errors. Read the warnings: new ones are your responsibility, not noise.
9. Run `npm run methodology` and commit the regenerated file with the data. If a rule changed, a row was added to the rule history: fill in the reason in the change log.
10. Run `npm run methodology:check` before shipping. It fails if the doc is out of date or if there is any error.

## 6. What updates itself, and what does not

Automatic: the counts, source tiers, freshness dates, next refresh date, open warnings, the rule table, the numbers table and the rule history in the AUTO sections;
the tests that run every rule on live data; the disclosure text (from the affiliate config); the image credits (from the manifest); the model-fit maths (from the data).

A person decides: what counts as a source, whether an outlier is an outlier, whether a rule is right, and what goes in the change log.
Schedule (to be automated when the site has a repo and a runner): weekly price refresh and `npm run methodology:check`; monthly model list review and `npm run models:links` (refreshes `src/data/models/links.json` from Hugging Face's public metadata; the audit warns after 30 days); quarterly review of this file.
Until a runner exists, whoever ships runs the check by hand.

## 7. When we measure something ourselves

A number is "measured by us" only if all of this is recorded next to it: hardware (every card, board, RAM, PSU), driver and OS versions, llama.cpp commit and backend flags,
model file and quantisation with its hash, context length, prompt and generation lengths, three runs with the median shown and the spread, power measured how and where, ambient room temperature
if throttling is possible, the date, and the exact command. Use `llama-bench` for speed so results are comparable with the published rows (pp512, tg128). Publish failures alongside successes.

## 8. Corrections and disputes

- We never silently change a number that was published. A change to a shown value goes in the change log with the date, the old value, the new value and why.
- If someone shows a source that contradicts a record, we check it within a week, and either fix it or reply with our source.
- A wrong recommendation (a build that fails) is treated as a defect in the checks, not a data typo: add a test first, then fix.
- Vendors and sellers cannot buy placement. Affiliate status never affects ranking (section 3).

## 9. Known limits

Asking prices are not what people paid. Community speeds come from other people's setups. Most model memory figures are our arithmetic. Several board slot lists were simplified
from extracted text. PSU and adapter entries are placeholders until real models are chosen. We have not benchmarked a multi-GPU build ourselves. The generated warnings below list the current gaps by id.
